MODULE 02

Types of Data & Variables

Understanding variables, levels of measurement and different types of clinical data used in health research.

Learning Objectives

  • Define data and variables.
  • Distinguish between qualitative and quantitative data.
  • Identify discrete and continuous variables.
  • Explain nominal, ordinal, interval and ratio levels of measurement.
  • Identify appropriate examples of variables used in clinical medicine.

1. Introduction to Data

Data are observations or measurements collected about individuals, patients, populations or other units of study. In clinical medicine, data are essential for describing patients, identifying disease patterns and evaluating treatments.

For example, a researcher may collect information about a patient's age, sex, blood pressure, HIV status, weight and treatment outcome.

2. What is a Variable?

A variable is a characteristic that can take different values among individuals or observations.

Clinical example:

If we collect the age of 100 patients, age is a variable because different patients may have different ages.

3. Qualitative and Quantitative Data

Qualitative Data

Qualitative data describe categories or characteristics rather than numerical measurements.

Examples:

Sex, marital status, disease status and blood group.

Quantitative Data

Quantitative data are numerical and represent quantities that can be measured or counted.

Examples:

Age, weight, height, blood pressure and number of clinic visits.

4. Discrete and Continuous Variables

Discrete Variables

Discrete variables are countable values, usually whole numbers.

Example: Number of children, number of hospital admissions and number of clinic visits.

Continuous Variables

Continuous variables can take any value within a range and can be measured with increasing precision.

Example: Weight, height, temperature and blood pressure.

5. Levels of Measurement

Understanding the level of measurement of a variable is important because it influences the statistical methods that can appropriately be used to analyse the data.

Level Description Clinical Example
Nominal Categories without an inherent order. Blood group
Ordinal Categories with a meaningful order. Disease severity
Interval Numerical scale with equal intervals but no true zero. Temperature in Celsius
Ratio Numerical scale with equal intervals and a true zero. Weight

6. Clinical Application

Consider a study investigating treatment outcomes among patients with hypertension.

Variable Type
Age Quantitative / Continuous
Sex Qualitative / Nominal
Blood pressure Quantitative / Continuous
Hypertension severity Qualitative / Ordinal
Number of clinic visits Quantitative / Discrete
